# Make A Mark: Impact Drive — Inclusive R1 Concept

## Purpose

Refine the R1 prototype so F1 fans with different interests and levels of familiarity can use it. The experience should work for someone who wants a quick interactive introduction, someone who wants to understand the engineering context, and someone who wants to inspect the evidence.

This document covers **R1 only**: one freight mission, the evidence library, an optional Race Engineer, and a small simulated Impact Rewards feature. It does not propose further product development after R1.

## Refined concept

**Make A Mark: Impact Drive is an F1 impact experience where fans can play, understand, verify, and see a simulated reward loop.** A short, fictional freight mission provides an engaging entry point. The hackathon R1 demonstrates a live-style session view with a scripted telemetry stream and illustrative impact placeholders; it does not connect to AMF1 systems or claim that placeholder values are real. Published AMF1 ESG records can provide factual, period-based context when cited. The optional AI Race Engineer explains the difference between these data types. A prototype-only Impact Rewards page demonstrates how game, video, and sustainable-merchandise actions could earn credits toward a tree-planting contribution.

The experience is not designed around age groups. It supports different motivations:

| User motivation | R1 response |
|---|---|
| Learn about F1 without already knowing the terminology | Use a short mission, plain language, and clear explanations of how freight relates to F1 operations. |
| Get a quick, visual introduction | Let the user start the freight mission directly and offer a concise takeaway. |
| Understand the operational or engineering trade-off | Explain the freight choice with a simple visual and optional detail grounded in the approved evidence. |
| Check whether a claim is credible | Show its source, reporting period, review status, and limitations; label any demonstration placeholder as illustrative. |

The game remains optional. Users can go directly to the evidence library, use the non-driving mission route, or ask the Race Engineer when they want help. Explanations can start concise and expand when the user asks for more; the product does not infer what someone needs from their age.

## R1 experience

1. **Choose how to begin:** enter the freight mission or explore the evidence library.
2. **Play the mission:** make a fictional freight route choice. Keep game values clearly separate from AMF1 facts.
3. **Understand the context:** explain why freight matters to an F1 team and what trade-off the fictional choice represents.
4. **Explore evidence:** open a cited published record to see the claim, source, reporting period, review state, and limitations; clearly distinguish it from illustrative demo placeholders.
5. **Ask the Race Engineer:** request a short explanation or a deeper account of a term, source, or supported comparison.
6. **Leave with a clear takeaway:** summarize what the user explored, without claiming that gameplay changed real-world impact.
7. **See the rewards concept:** eligible demo actions add one local Impact Credit; 10 credits can be exchanged for a simulated tree contribution. A yearly sample leaderboard and race-pass reward hypothesis make the engagement loop tangible.

Use readable text, plain-language definitions, visible keyboard focus, keyboard and touch controls, reduced motion, and an untimed, non-driving route throughout R1.

## R1 Impact Rewards feature

The rewards page is a **local prototype simulation**, not a carbon-credit scheme or a live AMF1 loyalty program:

- Completing the freight mission, recording a demo video-view completion, or simulating an eligible sustainable-merchandise purchase earns one Impact Credit.
- Ten Impact Credits redeem for one **demo tree contribution**. Credit and redemption state is saved only in the current browser; the prototype does not plant a tree, transfer funds, verify an order or watch, or issue a carbon offset.
- The page shows the user's demo redemption count on this browser, an **illustrative campaign total** placeholder, and a sample annual leaderboard. The campaign total is not a shared or verified planting count; sample fan accounts are fictional.
- A race pass for the annual leader is a prize hypothesis only. No AMF1 prize, contest, or eligibility terms are confirmed.
- Bamboo-based products and lower-impact shipping are examples for a possible eligible purchase rule, not claims about current AMF1 merchandise. The prototype has no shop or checkout integration.

If AMF1 chose to operate such a program, it would need a named planting partner, auditable contribution confirmations, a defined funding-per-tree mechanism, purchase/view completion validation, fraud controls, annual contest rules, and clear methodology for any environmental claims. Those are outside hackathon R1.

### What the available audience evidence says

- The 2025 Global F1 Fan Survey received more than 100,000 opt-in responses from self-identified, highly engaged fans across 186 countries. It reports that Gen Z made up 27% of respondents; 90% were emotionally invested in race outcomes; 61% engaged with F1 content daily; and 94% intended to follow F1 for five more years. Because respondents opted in through F1, Motorsport Network, teams, and affiliated media, this describes highly engaged fans, not all fans or casual viewers. ([F1 survey summary](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/formula-1-and-motorsport-network-unveil-2025-global-fan-survey.4YqMebNy8BLaapyJfjzDXO), [survey and methodology](https://fansurvey2025-formula1.motorsportnetwork.com/))
- Formula 1 reported in its 2026 half-year review that its global audience exceeds 830 million, with 43% under 35 and 42% female. This establishes broad audience reach and a large 35+ audience; it does not establish the addressable market for this particular product. ([Formula 1 2026 half-year review](https://corp.formula1.com/formula-1-2026-season-half-year-review/))
- The 2025 survey reports different discovery channels by segment: friends and family were strongest among women aged 25–34, while TV coverage was strongest among male fans aged 45+ and long-time fans. It also reports strong interest in participation and live experiences: 41% of surveyed fans who had not attended an F1 fan experience said they planned to attend one. These findings support multiple entry points and race-weekend use; they do not mean older users prefer long text or that fans specifically want this product. ([2025 Global F1 Fan Survey](https://fansurvey2025-formula1.motorsportnetwork.com/))
- The same survey found that 76% of respondents thought sponsors enhance F1 and one-third said they were more likely to consider an F1 partner's product. This is a sponsorship signal from a self-selected survey, not proof that a sponsor will fund Impact Drive or that the experience will cause purchases. ([2025 Global F1 Fan Survey](https://fansurvey2025-formula1.motorsportnetwork.com/))
- A 2019 F1 Fan Voice survey of 3,500 fans reported that 82% appreciated F1's sustainability efforts and 71% considered its strategy genuine. This is a positive but dated, self-selected signal, collected after F1 announced its net-zero goal; it has no published age breakdown and should not be used as current product-demand proof. ([Formula 1 sustainability fan survey](https://corp.formula1.com/fan-survey-shows-strong-support-for-f1s-sustainability-drive/))

### Interpretation for the four supplied personas

The evidence supports a broad F1 engagement opportunity, but not an assumption that all fans want an ESG game. The strongest current demand signals concern racing, stories, frequent content, and participation. Interest in sustainability exists, but the available direct sustainability-attitude survey is old; the 2025 survey does not establish sustainability, inclusion, or community impact as top fan-content needs.

R1 should therefore use the same experience with different user-selected depths, not age-specific product modes:

| Persona motivation | R1 response to validate |
|---|---|
| Chloe (18): beginner-friendly F1 learning and a shared interest with family | Make the freight/F1 connection understandable without prior jargon; provide a clear quick explanation. |
| Jayden (24): race-first, fast, visual, and trustworthy | Lead with the live-style demo view and short mission; make the report source and date easy to inspect. |
| Adam (38): engineering context and practical meaning | Explain what the simulated telemetry signals represent and what they do not; connect the mission to F1 operations using a sourced or clearly illustrative visual explanation. |
| Raymond (55): credible information and progress context | Keep source, timestamp, reporting period, definitions, and limitations visible; allow direct access to evidence without playing. |

These are design hypotheses based on supplied personas, not validated findings about age groups. The fan survey supports channel choices (for example, social/story-led discovery and broadcast/race-weekend context), not a fixed age-based interface.

### R1 desirability check

Before finalizing the R1 build, run a small concept and usability check with fans spanning 18–35, 36–54, and 55+, including newer and long-time fans. This is a directional check, not a representative survey. Ask participants to try the live-style demo view, understand one simulated telemetry indicator, complete or bypass the freight mission, and find the source behind one impact claim. Record completion, perceived relevance, correct interpretation of simulated versus reported data, source-finding, and willingness to use the experience again. Do not turn a small sample into population estimates.

## How R1 uses AI

AI turns cited impact records and a clearly labeled sample of the simulated telemetry stream into explanations that are easier to find, understand, and relate to the freight mission. It is called only when the user asks a question; it does not process or narrate every incoming demo update. It is a guide to the evidence, not the evidence owner.

### What the Race Engineer does

- **Finds relevant cited records** using the user's question and optional mission context.
- **Explains the latest demo telemetry state** with its simulated timestamp and signal definition when the question concerns the live-style session.
- **Explains technical terms** in plain language, with more detail available when requested.
- **Connects the question to the mission** without treating fictional game values as AMF1 results.
- **Summarizes what a source says and what it means**, while keeping those distinct.
- **Explains supported comparisons** only when reporting periods and methods are comparable; otherwise it explains why a comparison is not supported.
- **Returns the source trail**—document, edition, reporting period, page or location—along with relevant limitations; simulated values are labeled as demo data, never as AMF1 facts.
- **Declines unsupported questions** or says when the evidence is missing, disputed, illustrative, or not approved.

Example questions include:

- “What does freight mean in this mission?”
- “Why might a freight route affect emissions?”
- “What does the report say about this topic, and for which period?”
- “Can I compare these two reported figures?”

The Engineer can offer a concise explanation first and a more detailed explanation on request. It should not label users as beginner or expert based on age or build a personal profile.

### Evidence-to-answer flow

```text
Scripted demo telemetry stream
  -> local fixture / deterministic demo generator
  -> schema, unit, simulated timestamp, and freshness-state checks
  -> clearly labeled live-style session display

Published AMF1 ESG source + illustrative impact placeholders
  -> cited source records + visibly labeled demo-only values
  -> retrieval limited to sourced claims and approved demo content

User question + mission context + relevant simulated live-state summary
  -> server-side AI explanation
  -> answer with demo-data label and/or report citations, plus limitations
  -> user can inspect the signal definition or cited report record
```

Report citation details come from retrieved source records, not from text invented by the model. Demo values and simulated timestamps come from the prototype fixture, not model-generated prose. The model may explain this context, but it cannot change a record, its approval state, the mission rules, the mission score, or demo values.

### What AI does not do

- It does not control the car, choose a route, score the mission, or produce environmental outcomes.
- It does not make up missing values, citations, confidence scores, or comparisons.
- It does not present illustrative placeholders as AMF1 outcomes or infer emissions, inclusion outcomes, or community impact from car telemetry.
- It does not turn a target into an achieved result or hide a disputed figure.
- It does not treat general model knowledge as AMF1 evidence.
- It does not run continuously during gameplay or require a question to use the game or library.

If retrieval or the AI service is unavailable, the mission and evidence library remain usable. Any prepared response is labeled as prepared, not as live AI.

## Simulated live data in the R1 prototype

R1 is a hackathon prototype. It needs **no AMF1 telemetry access, API, credentials, or live-impact feed**. A scripted stream or local fixture can update the race-session view during the demo; impact cards can use clearly labeled placeholders to show how a future live impact view might look. Every screen containing synthetic values must visibly say **“Illustrative demo data — not live AMF1 data or a measured impact result.”** Use published AMF1 ESG claims as factual examples only when their source and reporting period are shown. The current [Make A Mark page](https://www.astonmartinf1.com/en-GB/make-a-mark) surfaces a 2025 report and current initiative highlights; use it as the preferred starting point for sourcing evidence, then verify every selected statement in its linked report.

This lets the prototype demonstrate the experience and AI interaction without claiming access to proprietary data or representing mock numbers as real. It does not prove that AMF1 currently provides live sustainability, inclusion, or community metrics. Car telemetry and impact metrics are different: a future AMF1 implementation would need approved impact data and methodology if the impact cards are to show actual live outcomes.

### R1 data architecture

```text
Local scripted telemetry fixture
  -> deterministic updates during demo
  -> signal mapping and simulated timestamp
  -> live-style session panel, prominently labeled illustrative

Published AMF1 ESG records + illustrative live-impact placeholders
  -> source-linked evidence library and placeholder cards
  -> AI retrieval when a user asks a question; placeholders remain labeled

User question + selected demo-state summary + relevant cited records
  -> optional Race Engineer answer
  -> simulated-data label and/or report citations, with limitations
```

The scripted stream updates the live-style session panel; it does not call the AI for every sample. On a user question, the app supplies only the latest demo summary relevant to that question, along with cited records. The AI explains the information but does not calculate environmental or social impact. Placeholder impact values are visual examples only, not computed or measured outcomes.

Keep three categories visibly distinct:

- **SIMULATED LIVE VIEW:** illustrative signal, unit, demo timestamp, and simulated freshness status.
- **REPORTED IMPACT:** published AMF1 claim, reporting period, source, and limitations.
- **ILLUSTRATIVE IMPACT PLACEHOLDER:** visibly fictional/sample value demonstrating a possible future live metric; never a reported AMF1 result.
- **MISSION SCENARIO:** fictional choice and deterministic game feedback; it never reads telemetry to decide the game score.

Use visible demo states for **updating**, **delayed**, **stale**, and **unavailable** so the prototype demonstrates feed handling. Mark demo timestamps as simulated. Show the claim's reporting period separately from any demo timestamp.

### Prototype data and trust rules

- No telemetry access request or AMF1 data integration is part of hackathon R1.
- Scripted telemetry and live-impact placeholders are clearly marked as illustrative everywhere they appear, including AI answers and screenshots.
- Cite real public AMF1 claims separately, with their actual reporting period; do not blend them with fictional live values.
- Do not describe the demo as a functioning AMF1 live feed or a real-time measurement of AMF1's sustainability performance.
- A future AMF1-owned implementation could replace placeholders with authorized feeds and approved metric methods; that is outside this R1 build and not a commitment to further development.

## R1 success criteria

- A first-time user can start with the mission or go directly to evidence without signing in.
- The freight mission is understandable without prior F1 or sustainability knowledge.
- The user can distinguish fictional mission results from AMF1 evidence.
- The live-style panel uses a scripted data fixture, labels data and timestamps as simulated, and demonstrates updating/stale/unavailable states.
- Placeholder impact metrics are visibly marked illustrative; published AMF1 claims show their source and actual reporting period.
- An AI answer, when available, uses only the user's question, relevant demo-state summary, and cited records, with clear demo labels and limitations.
- Unsupported questions receive a clear limitation or labeled prepared fallback.
- Simulated telemetry and impact placeholders are never described as actual AMF1 live data or impact results.
- Users can choose a concise or more detailed explanation without age-based profiling.
- Keyboard, touch, reduced-motion, untimed, and non-driving access remain available.
- No screen implies that a game action caused real-world impact or that demo data is actual/live AMF1 data.

## Evaluation readiness

The concept should be refined, not replaced, but its fit to the challenge is conditional. Hackathon R1 demonstrates a live-style **race context** through simulated telemetry and source-backed **impact information** through cited AMF1 ESG records and illustrative placeholders. Those are different data streams. Unless AMF1 later supplies an approved live sustainability, inclusion, or community-impact measure, the demo only partially demonstrates the challenge's “real-time view of impact” requirement. The scorecard asks for evidence of audience demand, market differentiation, revenue potential, and long-term operating viability. R1 remains the only build; the AMF1 ownership and funding model below is a proposal, not a commitment to develop a later release.

### Sharpened differentiator

F1 already offers [interactive games](https://www.formula1.com/en/page/gaming), an [AI fan companion for technical education](https://corp.formula1.com/formula-1-and-salesforce-expand-launch-of-new-fan-companion-agent-as-part-of-extended-partnership/), and sustainability-related fan activations such as the [Abu Dhabi GP's Race for the Planet](https://www.yasmarinacircuit.com/en/w/abu-dhabi-gp-app-game-2023-2). “An F1 game with an AI assistant” is therefore not distinctive by itself. Position the differentiator as:

> **An evidence-first F1 fan experience: a fan explores a freight decision, sees a live-style race view, and asks an AI Race Engineer to explain cited AMF1 impact records, with source, reporting period, and limitations visible. R1 demonstrates this with labeled sample data; an actual real-time impact view would require AMF1-provided and approved live impact data.**

The game is the invitation. The source-grounded evidence and explanation are the distinctive value. The single R1 freight mission remains the only playable mission. The library should expose the approved R1 evidence available across Environment, Belong, and Community; it must not imply complete coverage when the evidence subset is limited.

### Business opportunity and market potential

#### Audience and addressable market

Formula 1's reported global audience of more than 830 million is a reach indicator, not the number of people who would use this prototype or a revenue estimate. The reported 43% under 35 and 57% aged 35+ indicates that a fan experience should serve more than younger fans; the opt-in fan survey gives useful engagement signals but is not representative of every viewer. None of the available sources measures willingness to use or pay for an AMF1 sustainability experience. Do not multiply the global audience by an assumed conversion rate and present it as TAM.

Use a bottom-up market case once buyer discovery is possible:

```text
Annual opportunity = number of qualified team / event / sponsor activations
                     × evidenced price per activation

Serviceable opportunity = activations reachable through AMF1-owned channels
                          × evidenced eligible audience / engagement rate
                          × evidenced sponsor or internal funding per activation
```

The inputs still needed are: an identified budget owner, number of suitable annual activations, sponsor interest, acceptable procurement/production cost, and proof that target fans engage. Until those are known, there is no defensible monetary TAM/SAM/SOM. The opportunity is best described now as a **team- or event-sponsored fan engagement activation**, with commercial potential to validate, rather than a standalone consumer subscription market.

#### Proposed business model

**Primary model: B2B2C, free for fans.** AMF1 or an event partner commissions the experience as an engagement and sustainability-communication activation. A sponsor can underwrite production and event distribution in return for clearly labeled, limited brand placement. Fans use the mission, evidence library, and Race Engineer without paying or creating an account.

| Role | Proposed party | Value exchanged |
|---|---|---|
| User | F1 fans, including newer and long-time fans across ages | An engaging way to understand an F1 operation and inspect what the impact evidence does and does not say. |
| Buyer / accountable product owner | AMF1 fan engagement, communications, or sustainability team; exact budget owner to validate | A measurable fan activation that connects race interest with traceable impact communication. |
| Funder | AMF1 activation budget first; an event or commercial partner may co-fund | Funded experience and operation for an agreed campaign/event period. Sponsor participation is a hypothesis, not secured revenue. |
| Content/data approver | AMF1 sustainability and telemetry/data owners | Control of approved claims, data fields, wording, and release timing. |
| Delivery operator | A small digital product / agency team under AMF1 oversight | Prototype hosting, accessibility, content review, AI service, telemetry integration, event support, and reporting. |

Potential funding mechanisms to test are a fixed-fee event activation, an annual team-sponsored engagement package, or a co-funded sponsor activation. Keep any sponsor clearly identified and prevent sponsor review from changing the underlying evidence or conclusions. Avoid advertising based on personal data; R1 needs no account or individualized profile. A paid fan subscription is not supported by the current evidence and should not be the base case.

#### Cost base and commercial test

The main cost categories are AMF1 data access and security work, evidence preparation and human approval, prototype design/build, hosting and monitoring during the activation, AI inference, accessibility review, event support, and post-activation measurement. Telemetry rights or integration work may dominate uncertainty; obtain an estimate from the authorized feed owner before pricing. AI should run on user questions, not every telemetry update, to control usage and make answers reviewable.

The model is viable only if activation funding covers both one-time delivery and recurring operation:

```text
Contribution per activation = activation funding
                              − build / adaptation cost
                              − data-access and security cost
                              − content review and event operations
                              − hosting, monitoring, and AI usage
```

No reliable price, margin, or break-even figure can be stated without AMF1's delivery estimates. For this hackathon, present the buyer, funding route, and contribution formula as a reasoned business-model proposal, not as a secured contract or forecast. AMF1 would decide internally whether the accountable buyer sits in fan engagement/marketing, sustainability communications, or a partnership team, and whether an event or sponsor budget co-funds it.

### Proposed AMF1 operating and funding model

This is a **hypothesis for how AMF1 could own and fund the concept if it chose to take it forward**. It is context for the hackathon business case; it does not add post-R1 development to this team's scope.

- **Buyer / owner hypothesis:** AMF1's fan engagement or marketing team commissions the experience to deepen fan engagement; sustainability communications approves claims and sources. A commercial-partnership or event team could coordinate sponsor placement.
- **Funding hypothesis:** AMF1 pays from a fan activation or sustainability-communications budget, with optional sponsor/event underwriting. The fan experience remains free. Sponsorship is clearly labeled and does not control impact claims.
- **Operating responsibilities if adopted:** AMF1 supplies and approves content and any real data; its selected digital supplier hosts and supports the experience for an agreed activation period; sustainability reviewers approve claims; fan engagement owns event purpose and measurement.
- **Cost control:** answer questions on user request, limit AI usage, and use bounded approved data. In the hackathon prototype, local fixtures avoid data-access and feed integration costs; the business case separately acknowledges those costs for any AMF1-owned implementation.
- **Success evidence:** measure reach and engagement separately from actual impact outcomes. Track mission starts/completions, evidence opens, source-finding, answer usefulness, understanding of illustrative versus reported data, and accessibility issues. Do not claim that the prototype reduced emissions or changed behavior.

For the hackathon, this model demonstrates a plausible buyer and funding path; it is not proof that AMF1 has selected an owner or budget. Any future operational use would require AMF1 to assign those responsibilities and cover data rights, content review, security, hosting, AI, and event support.
